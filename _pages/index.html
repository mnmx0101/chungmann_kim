DEVELOPMENT ECONOMIST | HUMANITARIAN RESEARCHER | DATA SCIENTIST

Chungmann (Manny) Kim

Postdoctoral Research Fellow · Center for Humanitarian Health · Johns Hopkins University

I study how needs are measured and how policy and aid respond in development and humanitarian contexts.

  • Research. Food insecurity, conflict and displacement; their effects on nutrition and health; and how early-warning evidence shapes aid allocation. Quasi-experimental and spatial econometric methods.
  • Using AI. LLM and machine-learning methods where conventional data fall short, treated as measurement instruments.
    • Information extraction: LLM georeferencing and topic extraction from unstructured aid records, validated against official project locations.
    • Early warning: NLP anomaly detection on news, validated against conflict events.
    • Model governance: LACI, a framework for deciding when AI and statistical models are fit to inform global food-crisis classification.
    • Research workflow: agentic, version-controlled pipelines with human approval gates.

I completed my Ph.D. in Applied Economics at the University of Illinois Urbana‑Champaign. My work appears in Nature Food, Food Policy, Applied Economic Perspectives and Policy, Food Security and Food and Nutrition Bulletin. View Full Curriculum Vitae · Google Scholar

Nature Food 2025 · Lentz, E., Baylis, K., Michelson, H., & Kim, C.

Official Estimates of Global Food Insecurity Undercount Acute Hunger

Empirical investigation into global acute hunger monitoring systems and underreporting

+

Examines IPC subnational analyses across countries and years. Official estimates understate the severity of food crises and miss a substantial share of acutely hungry people, largely through under-classification around the threshold meant to trigger humanitarian response. Received the AAEA Quality of Research Discovery Award.

Inside the Black Box: How Consistent Are Global Food Security Crisis Analyses?

Evaluating internal consistency and methodological rigor across crisis assessment frameworks

+

Uses IPC subnational analyses across countries to recover how analysts weight evidence. Indicators are weighted differently across locations, so similar evidence can lead to different classifications.

Policy Work & Technical Projects

Policy Framework FAO–IPC GSU · 2025

LACI Framework: Evaluating AI Models for IPC Use

Learning, Assessing, Calibrating, and Integrating AI/ML model outputs

+

A review framework, developed with IPC and EC Joint Research Centre colleagues, for deciding when AI, machine-learning and statistical outputs are reliable enough to inform IPC classifications. It replaces numeric scores with qualitative descriptions of how models fail, and gives analysts screening cards (Learn and Assess) to work through before a model output enters an analysis.

GitHub Repository
Data-Driven Evaluation FAO–IPC GSU · 2025

Optimizing Classification Thresholds for Food Security Indicators

Household-survey harmonization and cross-country concordance

+

This project presents a data-driven approach to optimize classification thresholds used in food security monitoring systems. By evaluating pairwise concordance and agreement metrics across multiple indicators, the method seeks to enhance consistency and alignment between measurement tools across countries.

Early Warning with Kickstart AI & FAO · 2025

Unstructured News as Structured Risk: A Media-Based Pipeline for Anticipating Crises

Validated prototype that extracts early-warning signals from news

+

Asks whether news coverage signals food-crisis escalation earlier than official assessments. In South Sudan, keyword anomalies in news and field reports were validated against conflict-event data. A validated prototype built with Kickstart AI.
